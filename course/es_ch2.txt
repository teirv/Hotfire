=U slife | Daily Life | Family, routines and getting around.
=L sl1 | Family and possession
@T
`la madre` mother, `el padre` father, `los padres` parents, `el hijo` son, `la hija` daughter, `el hermano` brother, `la hermana` sister, `el esposo` husband, `la esposa` wife, `el abuelo` grandfather

Possessives: `mi` my, `tu` your, `su` his, her, their, `nuestro` our. Add `-s` for plural: `mis hijos`.

Spanish uses `de` where English uses 's: `la casa de Ana`, Ana's house.
@Q
How do you say "Ana's car"?
~opts
* `el coche de Ana`
- `Ana's coche`
- `Ana el coche`
~why
Spanish has no 's.
@B
Say "my children."
~code
___ hijos
~opts
* mis
- mi
- su
~why
Plural noun, plural possessive.
=L sl2 | Daily routine
@T
**Reflexive verbs** mean you do it to yourself. They use `me`, `te`, `se`, `nos`, `se`.

`Me levanto a las seis`: I get up at six

`Me ducho`: I shower

`Se viste`: she gets dressed

`Nos acostamos tarde`: we go to bed late

Frequency: `siempre` always, `a veces` sometimes, `nunca` never, `todos los días` every day.
@B
Say "I get up at seven."
~code
___ levanto a las siete.
~opts
* Me
- Te
- Se
~why
Yo goes with `me`.
@Q
What does `Nunca como carne` mean?
~opts
* I never eat meat
- I always eat meat
- I sometimes eat meat
~why
`nunca` means never.

=U spast | Talking About the Past | Two past tenses, and when to use each.
=L sp1 | Preterite: finished actions
@T
The **preterite** tells what happened, once and done.

`-ar`: `hablé`, `hablaste`, `habló`, `hablamos`, `hablaron`

`-er/-ir`: `comí`, `comiste`, `comió`, `comimos`, `comieron`

Time words: `ayer`, `anoche` last night, `la semana pasada` last week.

The accent matters: `hablo` is I speak, and `habló` is he spoke.
@B
Say "Yesterday I ate tacos."
~code
Ayer ___ tacos.
~opts
* comí
- como
- comió
~why
Preterite, yo form.
@Q
What's the difference between `hablo` and `habló`?
~opts
* I speak, and he spoke
- They mean the same thing
- He speaks, and I spoke
~why
Stress changes the meaning.
@W
Conjugate `hablar` in the preterite for ellos.
~accept
hablaron
~why
-aron for -ar verbs.
=L sp2 | Irregular preterite
@T
`ser` and `ir` share the same form: `fui`, `fuiste`, `fue`, `fuimos`, `fueron`. Context tells you which: `Fui al cine`, I went to the movies.

`tener`: `tuve`, `tuviste`, `tuvo`, `tuvimos`, `tuvieron`

`hacer`: `hice`, `hiciste`, `hizo`, `hicimos`, `hicieron`

`estar`: `estuve`, `estuviste`, `estuvo`, `estuvimos`, `estuvieron`

`decir`: `dije`, `dijiste`, `dijo`, `dijimos`, `dijeron`

These have no accent marks.
@B
Say "I went home."
~code
___ a casa.
~opts
* Fui
- Voy
- Fue
~why
Preterite of `ir`, yo.
@Q
What does `¿Qué hiciste ayer?` mean?
~opts
* What did you do yesterday?
- What are you doing tomorrow?
- Who did it yesterday?
~why
`hiciste` comes from `hacer`.
=L sp3 | Imperfect: how things were
@T
The **imperfect** describes the background: habits and how things used to be.

`-ar`: `hablaba`, `hablabas`, `hablaba`, `hablábamos`, `hablaban`

`-er/-ir`: `comía`, `comías`, `comía`, `comíamos`, `comían`

Irregular: `era` (ser), `iba` (ir), `veía` (ver).

`Cuando era niño, jugaba mucho`: when I was a kid, I played a lot.

Think of a movie: the imperfect is the scene, and the preterite is what happens next. `Llovía cuando llegué`: it was raining when I arrived.
@Q
Which one fits "I used to live in Mexico"?
~opts
* `Vivía en México`
- `Viví en México ayer`
- `Vivo en México`
~why
Past habits use the imperfect.
@B
Say "I was reading when she called."
~code
___ cuando ella llamó.
~opts
* Leía
- Leí
- Leo
~why
Background action uses the imperfect.
=U sfuture | Plans and Opinions | The future, the conditional and pronouns.
=L sf1 | Future and conditional
@T
**Future:** add endings to the whole infinitive: `hablaré`, `hablarás`, `hablará`, `hablaremos`, `hablarán`.

**Conditional (would):** `hablaría`, `hablarías`, `hablaría`, `hablaríamos`, `hablarían`.

Irregular stems for both: `tendr-`, `har-`, `podr-`, `saldr-`, `vendr-`, `dir-`, `querr-`.

`Mañana iré`: I'll go tomorrow. `Me gustaría viajar`: I'd like to travel.
@B
Say "I would like a coffee."
~code
Me ___ un café.
~opts
* gustaría
- gustará
- gusto
~why
Conditional for polite wishes.
@W
Put `tener` in the future for yo.
~accept
tendré
~why
Irregular stem `tendr-`.
=L sf2 | Object pronouns
@T
**Direct objects** replace the thing: `lo`, `la`, `los`, `las`. `Lo veo`: I see it.

**Indirect objects** say to whom: `me`, `te`, `le`, `nos`, `les`. `Le doy el libro`: I give him the book.

Pronouns go before a conjugated verb, or attach to an infinitive: `Quiero verlo`.

With both, indirect goes first: `Me lo das`. `le` + `lo` becomes `se lo`.
@B
Say "I see her."
~code
___ veo.
~opts
* La
- Le
- Lo
~why
She's the direct object.
@Q
Which sentence is correct?
~opts
* `Quiero comprarlo`
- `Quiero lo comprar`
- `Comprarlo quiero lo`
~why
It attaches to the infinitive, or comes before `quiero`.
=L sf3 | Commands
@T
**Tú commands:** use the él form: `Habla`, `Come`. Irregular: `ven`, `pon`, `haz`, `ten`, `di`, `sal`, `ve`.

**Negative tú:** swap the ending: `No hables`, `No comas`.

**Formal usted:** `Hable`, `Coma`.

Pronouns attach to positive commands: `Dímelo`, tell it to me.
@Q
How do you tell a friend "Eat!"?
~opts
* `¡Come!`
- `¡Coma!`
- `¡Comes!`
~why
Tú commands use the él form.
@B
Say "Don't talk!"
~code
¡No ___!
~opts
* hables
- hablas
- habla
~why
Negative commands swap the vowel.
=L sf4 | Por and para
@T
Both can mean "for".

**para** points to a goal: purpose, recipient, deadline, destination. `Es para ti`. `Estudio para aprender`.

**por** looks at cause and path: reason, exchange, duration, through. `Gracias por todo`. `Pagué diez dólares por esto`. `Caminé por el parque`.
@B
Say "This is for you."
~code
Esto es ___ ti.
~opts
* para
- por
- de
~why
A recipient uses `para`.
@B
Say "Thanks for everything."
~code
Gracias ___ todo.
~opts
* por
- para
- de
~why
A reason uses `por`.
@Q
Which one means "I study in order to learn"?
~opts
* `Estudio para aprender`
- `Estudio por aprender`
- `Estudio a aprender`
~why
Purpose uses `para`.
