=L spf1 | Present perfect
@T
**have + done:** `he`, `has`, `ha`, `hemos`, `han` + participle.

Participles: `-ar` → `-ado` (`hablado`), `-er/-ir` → `-ido` (`comido`).

Irregular: `hecho`, `dicho`, `visto`, `escrito`, `puesto`, `vuelto`, `abierto`, `roto`.

`¿Has comido?`: have you eaten?

`Nunca he estado en España`: I've never been to Spain.

Spain uses this a lot for today's events. Latin America often prefers the preterite.

=U ssubj | The Subjunctive | Wishes, doubts and feelings.
=L sj1 | Present subjunctive
@T
The **subjunctive** is a mood for things that aren't plain facts: wishes, doubts, feelings.

Take the yo form, drop `-o`, and swap vowels: `-ar` verbs get e, and `-er/-ir` verbs get a.

`hablar` → `hable`, `hables`, `hable`, `hablemos`, `hablen`

`comer` → `coma`, `comas`, `coma`, `comamos`, `coman`

`tener` → `tenga`. `hacer` → `haga`.

Irregular: `sea`, `esté`, `vaya`, `haya`, `sepa`, `dé`.

~accept

~why
-ar verbs swap to e.

It starts from the yo form, `tengo`.
=L sj2 | When to use it
@T
It usually follows `que` after a change of subject, triggered by WEIRDO:

**Wishes:** `Quiero que vengas`

**Emotion:** `Me alegra que estés aquí`

**Impersonal:** `Es importante que estudies`

**Recommendations:** `Te recomiendo que descanses`

**Doubt:** `Dudo que sea verdad`

**Ojalá:** `Ojalá llueva`

Facts use the indicative: `Creo que es verdad`. Doubt flips it: `No creo que sea verdad`.

`Cuando` about the future also takes it: `Cuando llegues, llámame`.

=U sadv | Advanced Patterns | Sounding more natural.
=L sa1 | If clauses and past subjunctive
@T
**Likely:** `Si llueve, no voy`: if it rains, I won't go.

**Unlikely:** `Si tuviera dinero, viajaría`: if I had money, I'd travel.

The **past subjunctive** comes from the preterite ellos form: `hablaron` → `hablara`, `tuvieron` → `tuviera`, `fueron` → `fuera`.

`Si yo fuera tú...`: if I were you...

=L sa2 | Everyday connectors
@T
`pero` but, `porque` because, `aunque` although, `entonces` so, then, `sin embargo` however, `además` besides, `mientras` while, `todavía` still, `ya` already, `antes de` before, `después de` after

**Natural fillers:** `bueno` well, `pues` well, `o sea` I mean, `¿verdad?` right?, `claro` of course

**Regional:** Mexico: `¿mande?` (pardon?), `ahorita` (right now, soon). Spain: `vale` (OK), `coche` (car). `carro` is common in Latin America.

=L sr2 | What comes next
@T
You've now seen the core of Spanish grammar. Fluency comes from using it, and Spanish is among the quicker languages for English speakers to learn.

**Vocabulary:** Learn the most common 2,000 words with a spaced repetition app like Anki.

**Listening:** Try slow Spanish podcasts, then shows and music you enjoy.

**Reading:** Start with graded readers, then news and novels.

**Speaking:** Talk every week. It's the fastest way to improve, and Texas gives you lots of chances.

**Testing yourself:** The DELE and SIELE exams use levels from A1 to C2.
