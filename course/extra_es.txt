=L xssound | Going deeper: stress and accents
@T
Spanish stress follows simple rules:

Words ending in a vowel, `n` or `s` stress the second-to-last syllable: `casa`, `hablan`.

Other words stress the last syllable: `hablar`, `ciudad`.

A written accent breaks the rule: `café`, `teléfono`. It can also change meaning: `si` (if) vs `sí` (yes).
@Q
Where's the stress in `ciudad`?
~opts
* The last syllable, `dad`
- The first syllable, `ciu`
~why
It ends in d, so the last syllable is stressed.
@Q
What does the accent change in `tú` vs `tu`?
~opts
* `tú` is "you", and `tu` is "your"
- Nothing, it's optional
- `tu` is louder
~why
Some accents only tell words apart.

@T
`cien` is exactly 100. `ciento` is for 101–199: `ciento veinte`.

`mil` is 1,000. `dos mil` 2,000. No "un" before mil.

`un millón de` personas: millón needs `de` before a noun.

Dates go day, then month: `el 3 de noviembre`. The 1st is often `el primero`.

~why

Both mean "to be".

**Ser** is what something is: identity, origin, job, time. `Soy ingeniero.`

**Estar** is how or where: feelings, location, condition. `Estoy cansado.` `Está en casa.`

Some words change meaning: `es aburrido` (is boring) vs `está aburrido` (is bored).

@Q

~opts

- `La fiesta está a las ocho.`
~why
Events use ser, even for location.

@T
Three types of "if":

**Likely:** `Si tengo tiempo, voy.` present plus present or future

@T
When reading Spanish:

Find the verb first. Its ending tells you who and when.

Look for cognates: `información`, `importante` and `problema` are easy wins.

Watch false friends: `embarazada` is pregnant, not embarrassed. `éxito` is success, not exit.

Read for gist first, then go back for details.

~why
