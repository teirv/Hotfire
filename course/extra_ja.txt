`〜らしい` based on what you heard: `{雨|あめ}らしい` apparently it'll rain.

Also: plain form plus `そうだ` means "I heard that": `{雨|あめ}が{降|ふ}るそうだ`.
@Q
You see a cake and want to say it looks delicious.
~opts
* `おいしそう`
- `おいしいらしい`
- `おいしいそうだ`
~why
Based on how it looks.

=L xjread | Going deeper: reading tips
@T
When you read real Japanese:

Find the verb at the end first. It tells you what's happening.

Particles split the sentence: は topic, が subject, を object, に or で place and time.

Kanji words are usually the content. Hiragana between them is grammar.

Don't stop at every unknown word. Get the gist, then go back.
@Q
Read `{毎朝|まいあさ}、{駅|えき}でコーヒーを{買|か}います。` What's the action?
~opts
* Buying coffee
- Going to the station
- Waking up
~why
The verb is `{買|か}います`.
