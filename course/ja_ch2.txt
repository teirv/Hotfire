=L js1 | Long vowels and small tsu
@T
You can read kana now. Here's what makes Japanese sound natural.

Each kana gets one even beat. **Long vowels** last two beats, and length changes meaning: おばさん (obasan) is aunt, and おばあさん (obaasan) is grandmother.

Long sounds are spelled by adding a vowel: ああ, いい, うう, ええ or えい, おう or おお. In katakana, a dash does it: コーヒー (koohii, coffee).

Tap any Japanese text to hear it.
@T
A small っ means a tiny pause before the next sound. It doubles the consonant in romaji.

きて (kite) means "come", and きって (kitte) means "stamp". がっこう (gakkou) means school.

ん is a full beat of its own: にほん is ni-ho-n, three beats.

=L jb1 | Sentences with です
@T
Japanese word order is different: **the verb comes last**. Small words called **particles** mark each word's job.

**A は B です** means "A is B." は marks the topic and is pronounced "wa" here.

Past: でした. Negative: じゃありません (or ではありません).

がくせいじゃありません。 I'm not a student.

=L jb2 | Questions and this or that
@T
Add **か** at the end to make a question. No word order change, and no question mark is needed.
