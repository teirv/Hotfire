=U jk1 | First Kanji | Characters that carry meaning.
=L jk11 | What kanji are
@T
**Kanji** are characters that carry meaning. A sentence uses all three scripts: kanji for core ideas, hiragana for grammar, katakana for foreign words.

Most kanji have two kinds of reading: **kun** (native Japanese) and **on** (from Chinese). Learn words, not isolated readings.

The small kana above kanji is called **furigana**. It's a pronunciation guide. Tap words to hear them.

{一|いち} one, {二|に} two, {三|さん} three, {四|よん} four, {五|ご} five, {六|ろく} six, {七|なな} seven, {八|はち} eight, {九|きゅう} nine, {十|じゅう} ten, {百|ひゃく} hundred, {千|せん} thousand, {万|まん} ten thousand, {円|えん} yen

=U jte | The て-Form | The most useful verb form in Japanese.
=L jt1 | Verb groups
@T
To change verb forms, you need the **three verb groups**.

**Group 2 (ichidan):** end in いる or える sounds. Drop る. {食|た}べる, {見|み}る, {寝|ね}る

**Group 1 (godan):** the rest. The last sound changes. {行|い}く, {飲|の}む, {話|はな}す, {買|か}う

**Irregular:** する do, くる come.

The basic form in a dictionary is the **plain form**.

Some look like Group 2 but are Group 1: {帰|かえ}る, {入|はい}る, {走|はし}る, {知|し}る, {切|き}る.
@Q
Which group is {飲|の}む?
~opts
* Group 1
- Group 2
- Irregular
~why
It doesn't end in る.
@Q
{帰|かえ}る looks like Group 2. What is it really?
~opts
* Group 1
- Group 2
- Irregular
~why
It's one of the exceptions. Its ます-form is {帰|かえ}ります.
=L jt2 | Making the て-form
@T
**Group 2:** drop る, add て. {食|た}べる → {食|た}べて

**Irregular:** する → して, くる → きて

**Group 1:**

う, つ, る → って ({買|か}う → {買|か}って)

む, ぶ, ぬ → んで ({飲|の}む → {飲|の}んで)

く → いて ({書|か}く → {書|か}いて)

ぐ → いで ({泳|およ}ぐ → {泳|およ}いで)

す → して ({話|はな}す → {話|はな}して)

Exception: {行|い}く → {行|い}って

- {読|よ}いて
~why
む becomes んで.
@Q

~opts
* {行|い}って
- {行|い}いて
- {行|い}んで
~why
It's the one exception.

=U jplain | Plain Form | How people talk to friends.
=L jp1 | Plain verbs
@T
Friends use the **plain form**, and it's needed for many grammar patterns.

**Negative (ない):** Group 2: {食|た}べない. Group 1: last sound → あ-row + ない: {飲|の}まない, {行|い}かない. う becomes わ: {買|か}わない. する → しない, くる → こない. ある → ない.

**Past (た):** same as て-form, with た: {食|た}べた, {飲|の}んだ, {行|い}った.

**Past negative:** なかった: {食|た}べなかった.

です becomes だ.

=L jm3 | Passive and causative
@T
**Passive (〜られる):** something is done to you. It often sounds like an annoyance.

{先生|せんせい}に {褒|ほ}められました。 I was praised by the teacher.

Group 1: あ-row + れる: {書|か}かれる. Group 2: られる: {見|み}られる.

Group 1: あ-row + せる. Group 2: させる. する → させる.

=L jm4 | Polite levels
@T
Japanese changes with the social situation.

**Casual:** {食|た}べる. For friends and family.

**Polite:** {食|た}べます. Your safe default.

**Honorific (そんけいご):** raises the other person. いらっしゃいます (is, goes), {召|め}し{上|あ}がります (eats), おっしゃいます (says).

**Humble (けんじょうご):** lowers yourself. {参|まい}ります (go), いただきます (receive), {申|もう}します (say).

Work and customer service use these all the time. Example: {田中|たなか}と {申|もう}します。 My name is Tanaka.

~why
It's humble. おっしゃいます is honorific, for others.

~why

=L jre2 | What comes next
@T
You've now seen the core of Japanese grammar. Reaching real fluency takes thousands of hours, and no single course covers it all. Here's what to do next.

**Vocabulary:** Use a spaced repetition app such as Anki, and aim for a steady number of words daily.

**Kanji:** About 2,000 are in everyday use, so learn them through words.

**Listening and reading:** Graded readers first, then NHK Easy News, anime, and podcasts.

**Speaking:** Weekly conversation practice is what makes it stick.

**Testing yourself:** The JLPT has five levels. N5 is the easiest, and N1 is the hardest.
